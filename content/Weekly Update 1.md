#### Felt Leadership through Caring Conversations

I had a 1 on 1 with a Leadership consultant at work who talked me through his framework for having effective field leadership conversations in the workplace. Essentially, this is a way to ensure that people are both physically and psychosocially safe at work. 

##### The framework starts with three pillars of Curiosity, Care and Courage.
**Curiosity:** Bring a learning mindset. Ask questions to develop shared understanding
**Care:** Make time and engage with the whole person to build trust. Actively listen to understand individual perspectives.
**Courage:** Allow for personal vulnerability and be prepared to challenge. You set standards through what you do, and what you don't do. 

*Focusing on these three pillars help to build trust. Once trust is established, then we move on to the Operational Controls. This is where we understand and plan the work, with robust control of risks and strong ownership.* 

##### Operational controls include:
**Work:** can you describe the work to me?
- What is the purpose and scope of work?
- What are the internal and external interfaces?
- What is going on around the task?
- What will completion / the result look like?
- Are there time constraints or pressures?
**Risk:** What do you see as the significant risks/threats/challenges?
- What are the hazards and critical risks?
- Are there developing or hidden hazards?
- Are there opportunities to avoid or eliminate risks?
- What are the critical risk controls?
**Plan:** Tell me about your plan for the work?
- Does the plan address Critical Risks?
- How robust are risk controls?
- Who was involved in creating the plan?
- What procedures and systems were used to create the plan?
- Who has the plan been communicated to and how?
- Does everyone who needs to know and understand the plan?
- Is everyone working to the same plan? Is there one plan?
**Own:** Who is in charge? Who is responsible for execution?
- What would trigger a stop and rethink?
- Do you feel comfortable taking a time out if things change?
- Who can you turn to if you have questions?
- Who is in control of the work?
- How are changes dealt with?
- How will the job be completed, closed out, left tidy?
- Are there actions, lessons, improvements? Who will do that?

##### A quick mental assessment of actions required can then be made:
- Are there positives to acknowledge?
- Are improvements required?
- What action can the participants take?
- What action can you take?

##### When leaving the conversation, self-reflection should be undertaken:
- Was it a caring conversation?
- Was there operational control?
- How were participants left feeling?
- What did I learn from this conversation?
- Do I need to follow-up?

Finally, the conversation should be recorded in both a ***companies digital system*** and into your own ***diary***. This helps to serve as evidence of effective leadership should there ever be an investigation into an incident in the workplace.
